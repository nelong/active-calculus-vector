Exercise 23.

You might recall the pythagorean identity from trigonometry given by
\begin{equation*} \cos^2(\theta)+\sin^2(\theta)=1 \end{equation*}
We want to use this identity to parameterize the circle with center \((h,k)\) and radius \(r \gt 0\) given by
\begin{equation*} (x-h)^2 + (y-k)^2 = r^2 \end{equation*}
🔗

(a)

Reorganize the general equation of a circle
\begin{equation*} (x-h)^2 + (y-k)^2 = r^2 \end{equation*}
to be of the form
\begin{equation*} (\underline{\hspace{2cm}})^2+(\underline{\hspace{2cm}})^2=1 \end{equation*}

🔗

(b)

Because identities work for any value, we can use the pythagorean identity to parameterize the general equation of a circle. Fill in the blanks below with each of the respective quantities from the previous task
\begin{align*} \cos(t) = \amp \underline{\hspace{2cm}} \\ \sin(t) = \amp \underline{\hspace{2cm}} \end{align*}

(c)

Solve each of the two equations from the previous part for \(x\) and \(y\) to get parametric equations for a generalized circle.
