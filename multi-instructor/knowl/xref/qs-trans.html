Exercise 22. Translating Center of Figure.

In this exercise, we will look at a few different surfaces and the algebraic forms used to translate the center or key feature of the surface. In particular, we will look at spheres, cones, and a parabolic cylinder surface with translations away from the origin.
🔗

(a)

For this part, we will be looking at transformations of the sphere given by \(x^2+y^2+z^2=1\text{.}\)
🔗

(i)

We need to start with a description of the key features of our surface. For a sphere, you need to state the center and radius since the surface is going to be complete described by these two features. Write a sentence that gives the center and radius for the sphere given by \(x^2+y^2+z^2=1\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

(ii)

Write a sentence that gives the center and radius for the sphere given by \((x-2)^2+y^2+z^2=1\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

(iii)

Give the equation for a sphere with center \((-4,2,-3)\) and radius 1.
🔗
🔗

(iv)

Generalize your work to give the equation of a sphere of radius 1 with center \((x_0,y_0,z_0)\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
🔗

(b)

For this part, we will be looking at transformations of the cone given by \(x^2+y^2=z^2\text{.}\)
🔗

(i)

We need to start with a description of the key features of our surface. For a cone, you need to state the center (where the vertex of the cone occurs) and the axis of symmetry that the cone is centered on, since the cone is going to be complete described by these two features. Write a sentence that gives the center and axis of symmetry for the cone given by \(x^2+y^2=z^2\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

(ii)

Write a sentence that gives the center and axis of symmetry for the cone given by \((x)^2+(y-2)^2=(z+1)^2\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

(iii)

Give the equation for a cone with axis of symmetry along the x-axis and center at the origin.
🔗
Hint.
Switch the roles of \(x\) and \(z\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
🔗

(iv)

Give the equation for a cone with axis of symmetry along the y-axis and center at \((1,-3,2)\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

(v)

Generalize your work to give the equation of cone with axis of symmetry along the x-axis and center at \((x_0,y_0,z_0)\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
🔗

(c)

For this part, we will be looking at transformations of the parabolic cylinder given by \(x^2=y\text{.}\)
🔗

(i)

We need to start with a description of the key features of our surface. For a parabolic cylinder surface, you need to state the plane with the generating curve and the features of the generating curve, since the surface is going to be complete described by these two features. Write a couple of sentences to describe this cylinder surface given by \(y=x^2\text{.}\) Be sure to include which plane contains the generating curve and the important features of the generating curve. You should also state which axis the rulings will be parallel to.
🔗
🔗

(ii)

Write a couple of sentences to describe the parabolic cylinder given by \(-(y+1)=(x-2)^2\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

(iii)

Write a couple of sentences to describe the parabolic cylinder given by \((x+3)=(z-1)^2\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

(iv)

Give the equation for a parabolic cylinder surface with generating curve in the yz-plane, with vertex of the generating parabola at \((0,-2,4)\text{,}\) and parabola extending in the negative z direction.
