part 12.3.1.b.

In single-variable calculus, you used integrals to find the volume of three-dimensional solids by slicing the solid into shapes (e.g., discs, washers, or cylindrical shells) whose volumes could be computed using formulas from geometry. We can do the same here by recognizing that a cross section of the tetrahedron parallel to the \(yz\)-plane, which is the intersection of the plane \(x=a\) with the tetrahedron, is a triangle.
