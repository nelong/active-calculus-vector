(a) The contour \(f(x,y)=1\) lies in the \(xy\)-plane and has equation
\begin{equation*} 4 e^{-(x-3)^2-y^2} = 1, \end{equation*}
so
\begin{equation*} -(x-3)^2-y^2 = \ln (1/4),\quad\mbox{or}\quad (x-3)^2+y^2 = \ln 4. \end{equation*}
This is the equation of a circle of radius \(\sqrt{\ln(4)}\) centered at \((3,0)\) in the \(xy\)-plane. Similarly, for \(k \lt 4\text{,}\) we have that the contours \(f(x,y) = k\) are the circles \((x - 3)^2 + y^2 = \ln(4/k)\text{,}\) all of which are centered on \((3,0)\text{.}\) (b) The cross-section has equation \(z=f(3,y)=4 e^{-y^2}\text{.}\) If \(x=3\text{,}\) the base region in the \(xy\)-plane extends from \(y=-\sqrt{27}\) to \(y=\sqrt{27}\text{.}\) Thus the area is
\begin{equation*} \hbox{Area } = \int_{-\sqrt{27}}^{\sqrt{27}}4 e^{-y^2}\,dy. \end{equation*}
(c) Using this idea and slicing parallel to the \(y\)-axis, we get
\begin{equation*} \hbox{Volume } = \int_{-6}^{6}\int_{-\sqrt{36-x^2}}^{\sqrt{36-x^2}} 4 e^{-(x-3)^2-y^2}\,dy\,dx. \end{equation*}
