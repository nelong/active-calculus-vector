\begin{equation*} \left|\frac{\partial (x,y,z)}{\partial (s,t,u)}\right| = \left|\begin{array}{ccc} x_s \amp x_t \amp x_u\\ y_s \amp y_t \amp y_u\\ z_s \amp z_t \amp z_u \end{array}\right| =\left|\begin{array}{ccc} 4 \amp -3 \amp -2\\ -2 \amp -1 \amp -4\\ -4 \amp 4 \amp 4 \end{array}\right|. \end{equation*}
This \(3 \times 3\) determinant is computed the same way as for the cross product, with the entries \(4,-3,-2\) in the first row playing the same role as \(\vec i,\vec j,\vec k\text{.}\) We get
\begin{equation*} \frac{\partial (x,y,z)}{\partial (s,t,u)}= ((-1)(4)-(-4)(4))4 + ((-4)(-4)-(-2)(4))-3 + ((-2)(4)-(-1)(-4))-2 = 0. \end{equation*}
