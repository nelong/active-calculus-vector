Preview Activity 13.9.1.
Recall the standard parameterization of the unit circle that is given by
\begin{equation*} x(t) = \cos(t) \ \ \ \ \text{ and } \ \ \ \ y(t) = \sin(t), \end{equation*}
where \(0 \le t \le 2\pi\text{.}\)
🔗
(a)
Give a parameterization of a circle of radius of 1 in \(\R^3\) that has its center at \((0,0,1)\) and lies in the plane \(z=1\text{.}\) Be sure to give the bounds on your parameter as well.
🔗
Hint.
The \(z\)-coordinate will be constant, so you only have to think about how \(x\) and \(y\) are changing in your parameterization.
🔗
🔗
🔗
(b)
Give a parameterization of a circle of radius of 5 in \(\R^3\) that has its center at \((0,0,5)\) and lies in the plane \(z=5\text{.}\) Be sure to give the bounds on your parameter as well.
🔗
🔗
(c)
Give a parameterization of a circle of radius of 1 in \(\R^3\) that has its center at \((0,0,-1)\) and lies in the plane \(z=-1\text{.}\) Be sure to give the bounds on your parameter as well.
🔗
🔗
(d)
What is the contour of the surface given by \(z^2=x^2+y^2\) for \(z=k\text{?}\) Write a few sentences to describe the shape and details of these contours in terms of the value of \(k\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
(e)
Use your work for the previous task to parameterize the contour of the surface given by \(z^2=x^2+y^2\) for \(z=k\text{.}\) Your parameterization should involve the parameter \(t\) and the value of \(k\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
(f)
Just as a cylinder can be viewed as a “stack” of circles of constant radius, a cone can be viewed as a stack of circles with varying radius. In particular, the cone given by \(z^2=x^2+y^2\) will have contours of circles centered on the z-axis with radius changing according to the rule \(r=z\text{.}\) Write a few sentences about why the component functions
\begin{equation*} x(s,t) = s \cos(t), \ \ \ \ y(s,t) = s \sin(t), \ \ \ \ \text{ and } \ \ \ \ z(s,t) = s, \end{equation*}
will parameterize the contours of \(z^2=x^2+y^2\text{.}\) Part of your explanation should describe what the values of \(t\) and \(s\) mean in terms of the cone \(z^2=x^2+y^2\text{.}\)
